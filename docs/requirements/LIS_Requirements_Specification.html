1. Introduction

1.1 Purpose

This document specifies the requirements of the new Laboratory Information System (LIS). It consolidates two inputs: the proven operational behaviour of LDM — National Technology's existing Oracle Forms laboratory system — and the intended product direction shown in the Sky LIS prototypes, a cloud, subscription-based, multi-tenant LIS with a central Super Admin portal and a per-laboratory Client portal. Every requirement is tagged with its origin so the reader can see what is baseline (present in both), what the new product adds, what LDM offers that the prototypes still lack, and what is recommended by analysis.

1.2 Scope

In scope: platform and tenancy, security and privileges, catalogue and master data, patient registration in all its modes, sample lifecycle, results and verification, reporting and printing, worklists and turnaround, finance, integration with analysers and external systems, auto-verification, dashboards, and the non-functional qualities of the system. Out of scope for this revision: installation and infrastructure procedures, detailed UI design, and the LDM modules the manual leaves undocumented (Tools, Quality Control, Sample Storage, SMS, Online Transfer, Outsourcing), which appear only as placeholders.

1.3 Audience

Product owner, solution architects, developers, QA, implementation and integration engineers, and laboratory domain experts who will validate the rules.

2. Product overview

2.1 Vision

The new LIS is a cloud, subscription-based, multi-tenant laboratory information system. National Technology operates a Super Admin portal for client lifecycle, internal users and global master data; each subscribed laboratory runs its daily work in its own Client portal with branches, roles, catalogue copy, payers and price lists, treasuries and integrations. Functionally it must preserve everything laboratories rely on in LDM — sample lifecycle, result semantics, status-driven queues, contract pricing, cash closing, claims, analyser interfacing and auto-verification — while adopting the prototypes' modern platform, security and user experience.

2.2 Actors

Phlebotomist / courier / representativeCollects samples (in the lab, at home, or from sending labs), scans collection, is recorded as collector.

Microbiologist / pathologistEnters culture results (organisms, antibiotics, sensitivities) and formatted text reports from templates.
Accounts officerMaintains price lists and contracts, issues claims to payers and sending labs, runs income and debtor reports.
Application / integration engineerBuilds the catalogue, privileges, analyser and PCR interfaces, and auto-verification rules; supports from logs.
Patient / payer / referring doctor (external)Receive reports (print, email, SMS, web portal), request quotes, settle balances.

3. Functional requirements

3.1 Platform & multi-tenancy (PLT)

Sky LIS is delivered as a cloud service. Each subscribed laboratory is an isolated tenant served through its own portal URL, while National Technology operates a Super Admin portal for the whole platform. LDM contributes the tenant-level configuration that governs daily behaviour.

LDM's privilege model is far more granular than the prototypes: rights per screen action, special rights for sensitive operations, and result rights per clinical group and branch. The prototypes add modern authentication flows. Both are required.

5. Business rules

Rules that must hold regardless of screen. Most are proven in LDM operation; they are listed so that no implementation choice can accidentally break them.

IDRuleStatementSource
BR-01Immutable identifiersPatient numbers are generated by the system and never change; accession numbers are sequential per visit; sample IDs are unique and never reused.LDM §3.1
BR-02Tube mergeServices with the same clinical group, sample type and sample condition share one sample; any difference splits them. Configuration, not the clerk, decides.LDM §3.2
BR-03Received gateA result may not be recorded until the sample status is Received.LDM §3.3
BR-04Profile completionA profile or package reaches Complete, Reviewed or Verified only when every member test has.LDM §3.3
BR-05Reserved keeps the billDeferring a service (Reserved) keeps it in the visit and the bill with no sample; deleting removes it from both.LDM §4.5
BR-06Contract sets the pricePayer and contract tier determine every service's patient share and payer share; a wrong contract produces a wrong invoice.LDM §3.4
BR-07Three-layer pricingBase list price × tier factor (patient) and × tier factor (payer) = contract price; Apply propagates base-list changes to all derived tiers except excluded services.LDM §10.2–10.6
BR-08Patient condition selects the rangeReference ranges resolve by sex, age and patient condition (and analyser when defined); the condition is captured at registration.LDM §3.5
BR-09Verify is a signatureVerification records who signed and when; only verified results print; unverified printing is an exception under explicit right.LDM §8.5, §8.8
BR-10Not-X queue logicIn status searches "Not X" returns everything below X in the test track (e.g. Not Verified = Ordered + Complete + Reviewed).LDM §8.9

BR-12Frequency checkA repeat order inside the configured interval alerts or is prevented, regardless of branch.LDM §11.7
BR-13Turnaround clockExpected result date = collection (or registration) time + duration counting only the test's running days; delay colours are a percentage of the duration consumed.LDM §9.2, §11.7

8.1 Glossary

TermDefinition
Accession numberSequential identifier of one visit (order); holds services, samples and the bill.
Clinical groupLaboratory unit/section (Haematology, Chemistry…); drives tube splitting, rights and report sections. LDM: Group.
Contract tierSub-category of a payer with its own price list (employees, families, cash). LDM: Relative.
Delta checkComparison of a result with the patient's previous value within a window; flags excessive change.
Job orderPrintout travelling with the tubes: services, units, samples, barcodes, collection signature. LDM: Sampling Details.
PackageSet of services across groups priced as one. LDM: Panel; a set of profiles printed as one report is a Mega Profile.
Panic (critical) valueResult beyond life-threatening limits requiring immediate notification.
Patient conditionAttribute (pregnant, diabetic, smoker) that selects an alternative reference range.
PayerBody paying for services: cash, company, syndicate, insurer, sending lab. LDM: Rank.
PreparationQuestion asked at registration about the patient's readiness (fasting, medication).
Received gateRule that no result can be entered before the sample is received at the unit.
ReservedDeferred service kept in the visit without a sample until its precaution is met.
Sample IDUnique barcode number of one tube.
ServiceOrderable catalogue item: a single test, a profile of tests, a package, a culture, a text report or an additional service.
TreasuryCash drawer at a branch, accessed by approved users from approved devices.
Turnaround (TAT)Patient TAT: registration → verification. Lab TAT: receipt → verification.
VerificationDoctor's sign-off (electronic signature) that releases a result for printing.
WorklistList of tests to perform, one row per test, filtered by collection date.
